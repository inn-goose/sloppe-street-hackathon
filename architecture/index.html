Sloppe Street

A forecasting agent for Agents vs Wall Street — twelve reported figures across Home Depot, Analog Devices, Hays plc and Deere & Company.

The system reads 1,139 filings into a verbatim store, extracts one row per stated fact, reconciles those facts into a period-keyed panel, builds several independent point forecasts ("anchors") for every target, and then decides which anchor to trust by backtesting that decision on the competition's own scoring rule.

The central design claim is that with four companies and ~1,300 usable observations, the winnable problem is not "learn the number" but "know which of several defensible estimates to submit". Every parameter that could have been chosen by taste is instead chosen by walk-forward evidence, and several plausible ideas were measured and rejected — those are listed in What was tried and abandoned.

Architecture

How a forecast is chosen

Each target carries several anchors — independent, parameter-free point forecasts of the same quantity:

anchorwhat it is
guide_midthe midpoint of the filer's own stated range for exactly this period
seasonal_last / seasonal_recentan annual guide split by the quarter's measured share of the year
companion_lag1a guided sibling line times the historical ratio between them
stated_growtha level the filer never states, from a growth rate it does
yoy_carry, prior_year, lag1, runrate_seasonalhistory-only baselines, available almost everywhere

The model layer reshapes this into one training example per (row, anchor) pair and learns the residual — y/anchor − 1 for money and per-share lines, y − anchor in points for rates. Pooling this way turns 1,327 rows into 4,604 examples and lets a Deere observation inform a Home Depot one, which is the only way to get a usable sample size from four companies.

Ten strategies were then run walk-forward over seven expanding folds, cut on the date each figure was published, and scored on the competition's own rule:

The guide range-position variant scores marginally better than the adopted guide-bias one, but the two are indistinguishable where they differ (t = +0.85, 13 rows better against 14 worse) while guide-bias has the stronger evidence against the un-corrected baseline. The simpler, better-evidenced one is used.

Selection beat correction — and the gradient-boosted model is how we know. Pooled over every anchor, LightGBM is clearly better than leaving anchors alone (median normalised error 0.121 vs 0.146): it repairs last-quarter's-value from 0.169 → 0.115 and last-year's-quarter from 0.160 → 0.110. But those are the anchors a row is not forecast from. On the anchors that actually win selection it is harmful — the seasonal split degrades from 0.071 → 0.134 and the guide midpoint from 0.072 → 0.172. A good anchor is already calibrated. The model earns its place in the repository by establishing that, not by moving a submitted number.

The one deliberate deviation

Submitting a guide midpoint scores about 1.0 by construction, because that is where the sell side also sits. Filers systematically clear their own guides — ADI has beaten its revenue guide in 88% of 34 observations by a median 2.5% — so the midpoint is shifted by that measured median. Paired against the un-shifted rule on target metrics: t = −2.21, 21 rows improved against 8 worsened. Shifting every anchor the same way is not significant (t = −1.18), so only the guide is shifted.

Two of these changed submitted numbers. The additivity identity exposed a fiscal year mislabelled by twelve months — Home Depot's 2025-02-02 quarter was filed as FY2025Q4 by one document and FY2024Q4 by another, and the contested label won, so one year lost a quarter and the next carried two. The containment rule found a Hays annual net-fee figure of £534.2m against a first half of £651.9m, with the correct £1,189.4m sitting in the candidate set, outvoted three to one.

What was tried and abandoned

The most serious mistake, and the one that shaped the architecture. An earlier version computed forecasts directly in the feature layer as hand-written bridges, with constants like a four-quarter averaging window and a bare × 0.97 in the source. Those constants were tuned by watching the printed value move toward the sell-side consensus — which is fitting on the benchmark being competed against. Because such a bridge produces exactly one number for one period, it has no history and no backtest can ever catch it. The whole layer was rebuilt so that every rule is computable at every past period and has to earn its place out of sample. The feature layer now emits series only; every parameter lives in the model layer.

Measured and rejected on evidence, rather than dropped on taste:

  • GBM correction of the chosen anchor — helps pooled, harms the anchors actually used (0.095 → 0.113 mean score 3.783 → 4.117).
  • Blending anchors by inverse variance — anchor quality varies far more than blending can absorb (3.885 vs 3.783).
  • Shrinking the chosen anchor toward the anchor consensus — tightens the upper quartile (0.341 vs 0.363) but costs mean score (3.824 vs 3.783), and mean score is the prize.
  • A companion-residual anchor (guide minus year-to-date, split by the remaining quarters). First measured as worse than the simpler share route and set aside — then, once fold ordering was corrected (below), it won on its own evidence and now carries Deere's diluted EPS. The first measurement was made on contaminated timestamps; the idea was right and the evidence was not.
  • A macro-regression anchor for HD comparable sales from the building-materials retail tape. Correlation is +0.93, but its median residual is 2.39pp against the existing anchor's 1.55pp — the correlation is carried by pandemic-era extremes and adds noise in the current regime.
  • ADI gross margin regressed on revenue scale — full history gives 4.86pp. A recent-window fit looks far better, but that residual is in-sample and choosing the window by fit is the same error as the bridges above.
  • Deere's segment's operating margin guidance — deliberately not bound. Deere states three segments' margins in one release with an empty period field, so nothing in the row says which segment it belongs to.

A contaminated backtest clock, found late and worth flagging. Folds were ordered by each figure's latest mention, which is when a value was last restated — and vendor lanes stamp their rows with the fetch date. The median gap between a quarter's end and that timestamp was 387 days, with 28% of rows beyond 400 days, so the "expanding window" was partly sorted by when data was downloaded rather than published. Re-keyed to the earliest publication, floored at the period end, the median gap is 23 days and 4.7% exceed 400.

The headline conclusions survived unchanged (adopted strategy 3.745 → 3.742, GBM still worse), so the leak was not driving the decision — but two forecasts moved, and the companion-residual anchor went from rejected to adopted. It is listed here because a backtest that cannot be trusted on its clock cannot be trusted at all, and the fix is the reason to believe the rest.

Known weaknesses

  • Four of twelve rest on weak anchors. HD comparable sales (σ 1.55pp) and ADI adjusted gross margin (σ 1.00pp) are measured against a 0.5pp scoring floor, so a poor score there is likely whatever method is used — neither is guided by its filer. Deere's diluted EPS (σ 0.225) reaches its target through a companion line rather than a guide.
  • Hays' tightest number rests on three observations. The stated-growth anchor shows σ 0.011, but that is a median over three historical years, one of which is 62% wrong because its base year is still unreliable. The reasoning is near-arithmetic and we trust it; the measured confidence is thinner than the number suggests.
  • 20 of 153 additivity pairs still fail. Deere's pre-2018 quarters are contaminated with year-to-date columns. Those years are dropped from seasonality but still feed lag columns on old training rows.
  • 43 stated guides could not be resolved to a period and are refused rather than guessed at.
  • Seven of twelve deviate materially from the available benchmark. That is the only way to score below 1.0, but the loss caps at 5.0 while the floor is 0 — a bad miss costs roughly eight times what an equal gain earns.

Reproducing the run

git clone <this repo> && cd <repo>
git clone https://github.com/kernlai/agents-vs-wall-street-starter.git starter   # corpus, not redistributed
python -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
brew install libomp            # LightGBM's OpenMP runtime, macOS only

PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python -m code.run all      # fetch → extract → feature → model

The final command writes submission/ADI-FY2026Q3.xlsx, DE-FY2026Q3.xlsx, HAS-FY2026.xlsx and HD-FY2026Q2.xlsx, and prints each cell it wrote after reading the saved files back from disk. A timestamped clear-run log is in logs/.

The document corpus is third-party material licensed to the organisers and is not redistributed in this repository; data/ is likewise excluded because it holds verbatim copies of those filings, and is fully regenerated by the command above.
